# Automation triggers

Discover the trigger before writing it: `signal_read` view catalogue with a query gives each trigger with its source, kind, field, match keys and how to use it. A signal kind, match key or value that was not discovered there does not exist, so an invented one never fires.

## Build from real contracts

Discover before writing: `automation_read` view `catalog` (filter `query`, `group` or `kind`) lists node kinds with their ports, types and examples, plus Custom Nodes. Use only kinds and port names it shows. Read `device_read` for every device control a node uses. Read `signal_read` with view `catalogue` and a `query` (a device, a room, a plugin or what you want to react to, optionally a `group`) to find the trigger: each row gives its source and kind, the ways to use it (`how`), its field and match keys and a plain-language sentence. View `kinds` lists every signal kind with its match values, then view `values` gives current examples. Never invent a signal kind, a match key or a trigger from a device name. A computer is a device: its readings (battery level, processor and graphics load and temperature, memory and disk use, network rates, locked, front app, display count on) and controls (volume, mute, audio output, microphone mute, brightness, night light, Do Not Disturb, power mode, playback, power actions) are capability keys in `device_read`. Per-app sound and per-screen changes have no key: they are the `computer.audio` and `computer.display` nodes.

A comparison against a missing value or a value of another kind fails the run: that means the home data is missing, so say so instead of working around it.

A match key with `values: null` has no fixed list and takes exact observed `data` or `details` values. Keep fixed lists for enumerated keys. Empty latest values do not prove a trigger unsupported. For camera sound events keep the discovered detection (Baby crying, Unusual sound, Loud sound are distinct; plain Sound is broad).

Computer app events: the events `app_opened` (an app gets its first window), `app_closed` (its last window goes), `app_focused` (it comes to the front) and `app_unfocused` (it leaves the front) carry the match keys `app` (name), `appId`, `title`, `user` and `personId`, and `event_find` reads them. To wait for an app to stay open or in front, trigger on the `computer` signals `app_open` or `app_focus` (value true or false, `field` the app id, `subject` the computer, match `app`) with test `{is: eq, to: true}` and `hold`, for example Firefox open for 30 minutes is `hold` 1800; each app is tracked on its own. They need the computer's `app_events` permission together with `apps` (on by default, owner only, listed in `computer_read` `permissions`) and are reported only by Linux computers with KDE or X11 window control.

Provider events with no named kind: nothing a device reports as an event is dropped. A Xiaomi event the home does not name, a SmartThings attribute that is not shown as a device value, a camera event-list type that is not a known detection and a read-only Tuya alert data point (alarm, alert, warning, fault, tamper, siren, trigger) that turned active all arrive as `activity` events. They carry a plain `summary`, and their own facts: `event` and `miotEvent` (Xiaomi name) with `arguments` (its values, not matchable), `attribute`, `capability`, `topic` and `component` (SmartThings), `code` and `value` (Tuya), `eventType` and `hasClip` (cameras). Read them with `event_find` and trigger on `source: event`, `kind: activity` with any of these as match keys. Camera detections also carry `source`, `eventType`, `hasClip`, `fileId` and `imgStoreId`, and appliance events `family`, `item` and `state`. Plain on/off and number changes are device values, not events: trigger on those with the `device` signals.

Activity feed for any signal: a signal kind marked `feed` in `signal_read` (plugin events by default, hub notices such as `storm`) is also recorded once as a `event_find` row of kind `signal`, with `details` source, kind, field, label, subject, value, `profileId` when it belongs to one person and `data` when it has any. Narrow those rows with `event_find` `source` (the signal source, for example `hub` or `plugin_router`) and `signalKind`; a person's AI sees only their own rows. A plugin author can set `feed: false` on a manifest event to keep it out of the feed; it still triggers automations.

Wake, listening and calls: the `listening` signals `wake_heard` (once per settled wake contest; `subject` the winning camera, phone, computer or channel id, or `discord`; `value` its loudness in dBFS; match `source`, `winner` and `decidedBy` only, capped at 6 a minute with a burst of 3 per source) and `state` (per listening source: `value` is `on`, `off`, `paused:<reason>`, `budget_used_up`, `mic_permission_lost`, `no_host` or `offline`; match `state`, `source` and `reason` privacy, in_call, discord_call or no_openrouter_key; camera transcript pauses are not reported) and the `call` signals `started` and `ended` (`subject` and `profileId` the person; match `source` chat, orb, palette, assist, assistant, shortcut or hosted, plus `person` and `device`; `ended` also `ending` and its `value` is the length in seconds, so test `gt` with 600 for calls over ten minutes) and `outreach` (`value` and match `state`: ringing, answered, declined, missed, failed or sent, plus `how` and `target`; trigger on `ringing` then `missed` to catch an unanswered AI call). All of them are recorded as `signal` rows in `event_find`, `started` and `outreach` survive a flood, none carries what was said, and the owner can switch the `listening` or `call` source off with `updateTriggerSettings`.

People arriving and leaving: the router plugin's signals `person_arrived` and `person_left` (source `plugin_router`, `subject` the person's name; match `name`, `person_id` and `profile_id`) fire once when a person's first linked device or paired phone shows them home, and once when the last one has been gone past the router's grace period. They are recorded in `event_find` like any plugin signal and capped at 6 a minute per person. As a condition, the router device has `raw:people_home` (how many are home) and one boolean `raw:home_<person id>` per tracked person (person ids come from `who_is_home`), readable with `device_read` and usable in `compare` like any control. Presence never selects the server route.

Flood protection: every signal source has a rate cap per device, field or kind, and a signal repeating faster than its cap is dropped, so never build a trigger on a continuous reading by itself: use a window (avg, min, max, count), a comparison with `hold`, or a crossing. Excess is logged and the `hub` signal `storm` (match `source`, `kind`, `reason` cap or global) tells the home when it happened; alarms and rings (`urgent` kinds) survive a flood. The owner switches a whole source or one `source/kind` off, or on for opt-in kinds (`optIn`), with `operation_call` `updateTriggerSettings` (`sources`: name to true or false, `kinds`: `source/kind` to true or false, null restores the default); `settings_read` shows `trigger_settings`, and an off kind drops its signals without a trace, so check it before blaming a trigger that never fires.

Commands and service connections: every command sent to a device is a `device` signal `command` (`subject` the device, `field` the control key, value what was sent) with match `status` (`sent`, `done`, `failed`, `timed_out`), `capability`, `cause` (`app`, `assistant`, `automation`, `scene`) and, when it failed, `error`; only `failed` and `timed_out` reach the activity feed (`event_find` kind `signal`), so trigger on `sent` or `done` for the rest. A connection to a service (Xiaomi, Tuya, SmartThings, Samsung TV, local) is the `provider` signal `status` (value and match `state`: `linked`, `attention` when it needs signing in again, `down` when it cannot be reached; also `provider`, `problem`, `error`). Each outage and each new adapter failure is one `provider_error` event, never one per device: while a service is in `attention` or `down`, its devices' `device_offline` and `device_online` events are not recorded, so read the `provider` status instead (the device itself still shows offline).

Device values, online and lifecycle: every `device` signal `state` also carries the facts of its device as match keys, so one trigger covers a whole group with no device list: `family` (light, fan, lock, computer, ...), `kind`, `roomId`, `provider`, `name`, `role` (what the value is, shared across models, such as `power`, `brightness`, `fault`), `unit` and `cause` (`physical` when someone used the device itself, `app`, `assistant`, `automation` or `schedule`, read from the command that preceded the change). Any light turning on in the Study is `match` family light and roomId of that room, `field` `power`, test `{is: eq, to: true}`; only a wall switch flip is `cause` physical. A value turning unknown is a signal with value null (an alarm cleared, playback stopped); the first value a device ever reports is not a signal. Numbers are announced only when they moved by at least the control's step and at most every 2 seconds (every 10 seconds for graphed readings such as latency), and the newest value follows when that pause ends, so use a window or `hold` for slow drift. `device` signal `online` (value true or false, every device including a computer, match the same facts; the activity feed keeps `device_online` and `device_offline` events) gives `hold` of 10 minutes on `eq` false for offline for ten minutes. `device` signal `lifecycle` is a moment with match `change`: `added`, `removed`, `renamed` (data `from`, `to`), `moved` (room), `replaced` (other hardware, `from`, `to` model), `capabilities_changed` (`added`, `removed` control keys) and `firmware_changed`; it reaches the activity feed as `signal` rows. Renames and moves the person makes in the app are not lifecycle signals.

Water bottle events: a LARQ bottle sends `activity` events whose `activity` is drink, refill, uv_clean, goal_reached or drinks_imported (a drink carries `ml` and `levelMl`; imported drinks older than 30 minutes arrive as one drinks_imported event), a `fault` event with the `fault` code and `name`, and `maintenance` events whose `maintenance` is battery_low or filter_due. Its `water_level`, `bottle_volume`, `battery`, `bottle_charging` and `bottle_state` are `device` state signals and conditions (no node reads the day's water total, so use the bottle level or the goal_reached event). Nodes can set `reminder_colour`, `reminder_interval`, `quiet_hours` and the window times (applied when the bottle next wakes) and `uv_clean` stop; an automation can never start a UV clean.

Treadmill events: a WalkingPad sends `activity` events whose `phase` is started, paused, resumed, finished or goal_reached (match `walker`, `kind` walk, jog, run or intervals, `goal`; the finished event also carries `distance_m`, `duration_s` and `steps`, which are read with `event_find` and cannot be matched), a `fault` event with the `code` (E01 to E13, or safety_stop), a `maintenance` event with `reason` lubrication_due, and a `button` event with `button` up, down, stop or mode. Its belt state, speed, mode and child lock are `device` state signals (use `hold` or a crossing for speed). A `device.set` node that starts the belt or raises its speed is refused like any remote start unless the owner allowed remote start, so keep stop and pause nodes for safety automations and tell the person when a start node will be refused. A Kingsmith G15 also has `belt_incline` as a `device` state signal and condition (the belt's own number, whole steps): trigger on it only with a `gte` or `lte` test, `hold` and `rearm` for hysteresis (for example `gte` 8, `rearm` 6, `hold` of 20 seconds), never on every change. A `device.set` node on `belt_incline` is refused unless the owner allowed remote incline, only raises up to the remote cap, and never runs while nobody walks, so do not build an automation that depends on it. The hub raises `goal_reached` only for the goals the belt supports today (distance, calories, time); there is no climb target event yet, so do not promise a notice for a reached climb target. There is no power off or standby step.

Toothbrush events (Philips Sonicare and Oral-B): the brush sends an `activity` event when a brushing is saved, with `brushing` finished (match `complete` true or false for a whole routine, `person` by name, `mode` and `durationS`), a `maintenance` event whose `brushHead` is warn or due when the brush head wears out, and a `fault` event with `brushFault` (error bits in hexadecimal). Its `brush_activity` (asleep, standby, brushing, paused, charging, off), `brush_mode`, `battery` and the head figure (`brush_head_wear` on a Sonicare, `brush_head_days_left` on an Oral-B; use a crossing) are `device` state signals and conditions. Activity and pickup changes are seen only while the brush's adverts reach a phone, so a trigger on them can be late. A `device.set` node can change the mode (and, on a Sonicare, intensity and the feedback switches) only on a handle that writes, with the owner's consent, and while it is awake and not brushing; it never starts a brush.

Printer events: the printer plugin is the signal source `plugin_printer`. Its kinds are `job_started` and `job_finished` (match `result` such as completed, canceled or aborted, plus `person` and `pages`), `problem_added` and `problem_cleared` (match `keyword` such as media-jam-error, `severity` and `part`), `alert`, `supply_low`, `supply_empty` and `supply_replaced` (match `supply` and `key`; the level is in the data), `restarted`, `config_changed`, `pairing_problem` (match `reason`) and `offline_long` (30 minutes without an answer). Every event carries the printer name in `printer` and its id in `printer_id`; match `printer` to pick one printer. Low and empty events follow the owner's notification switches and the low threshold set on the printer, and a flood is capped. Its state, queue, pages today, supplies and trays are `device` state signals.

Speech triggers (`trigger.speech`) match literal words or a contiguous phrase of finalized speech. `signal_read` view speech_sources shows the cameras, plugins and chats that can be chosen; leave `source` out for every authorized source. Bot speech is ignored unless `includeBots` is set. Only the person who saved a speech automation may change it.

## Triggers and timing

Triggers are entry nodes: `trigger.device` (one device or a whole room, group or family), `trigger.signal`, `trigger.time`, `trigger.interval`, `trigger.sun`, `trigger.speech`, `trigger.presence`, `trigger.camera`, `trigger.button`, `trigger.called`, `trigger.value`, the run-status trigger, the device-health trigger and the eleven later ones listed below; the catalog lists each one's outputs, and a node reads them with a wire from `trigger` and the output name, together with the ports `key` (which trigger fired) and `at`. A device or signal trigger takes a `test` (`eq`, `ne`, `gt`, `gte`, `lt`, `lte`, `between`, `in`, `changed`), a `window` (average, min, max, count or rate over a time) and these timing ports, all durations unless noted:

- `hold`: the test must stay true that long. `debounce`: decide only after no new signal for that long. `cooldown`: fire at most once in that long.
- `silentFor` (1 second to 7 days) fires when no matching signal arrived for that long, for example no motion in the hallway for 2 hours; it starts counting when the automation is enabled, every matching signal restarts it, it survives a hub restart, fires once per silence, and takes only `cooldown` besides it.
- `first` (`day`, `week` or `ever`) lets only the first match in the home day, the home week (Monday start) or ever fire, remembered across restarts; a match held back by `cooldown` does not use it up.
- `rearm` is a number for hysteresis on a `gt`, `gte`, `lt` or `lte` test: after firing on `gt` 28 with `rearm` 26 it fires again only after the value went below 26 (for `lt` and `lte` above it); `rearm` lies on the far side of the test value.
- `ignoreOwnChanges` (on by default) keeps an automation from triggering on its own commands; leave it on unless the person wants the loop.

The later triggers:

- `trigger.becomes_true` takes a `condition` (a wire to a flag from the read and calculation steps at the very start of the program) and an optional `hold`. It is checked only when something the condition reads changes, never on a timer, and fires on the change from false to true: not when the condition is already true as the automation is saved or the hub starts, and with `hold` only after it stayed true that long. A condition that fails, for example on a missing value, counts as false and the reason is logged.
- `trigger.computer` (`computer`, `kind` `app_open`, `app_focus`, `app_close`, `app_unfocus`, `power` or `power_<action>` such as `power_lock_screen`, plus `app` and `title`), `trigger.phone` (`phone`, `kind` `unlocked`, `context_changed` or `parked`, plus `to` and `from` for a change of activity), `trigger.health` (`kind` `sleep_ended`, `body_reading`, `today` or `logged`, plus `person`, `metric` and the timing ports) and `trigger.chat` (`chat`, `outcome` `answered` or `failed`) start on what the hub already sees.
- `trigger.weather` starts the moment new weather arrives and something changed (no change, no start; the feed refreshes every 15 minutes, the step of the provider's current data). `event` is `changed` (a `metric` such as temperature, feels_like, humidity, precipitation, wind_speed, uv_index or aqi, with a `test` and, to fire once per crossing, `rearm` a little past it on the other side, for example test above 25 and rearm 24), `band` (a `metric` moving into another band, `to` and `from`: temperature and feels_like freezing, cold, cool, mild, warm or hot; humidity dry, comfortable or humid; precipitation none, light, moderate or heavy; cloud_cover clear, partly_cloudy or overcast; wind_speed calm, breezy, windy, gale or storm; uv_index low, moderate, high, very_high or extreme; aqi good, fair, moderate, poor, bad or severe), `condition` (`to` and `from` clear, partly_cloudy, cloudy, fog, drizzle, rain, snow or storm), `rain_started` (optional `falling`), `rain_stopped`, `rain_expected` (`within` 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 3h or 6h, judged from the hourly forecast) or `forecast` (every update). For rain use `rain_started` and `rain_stopped`, never a polling interval with a weather read.
- `trigger.event` starts on the `signal.emit` step that sends the same `name`; `trigger.timer` starts when the Timer step of that `name` ends, also when the hub was down at the time; `trigger.hub_started` starts once each time the hub starts; `trigger.schedule` starts at each block of a weekly plan.
- `trigger.webhook` is for owners. Its `path` is the secret: 16 to 128 letters, digits, dashes or underscores, and the address is `/hooks/<path>` on the hub (GET, POST, PUT or DELETE, or the one `method` chosen). The run reads `trigger.method`, `trigger.body`, `trigger.headers` and `trigger.query`. An `http.respond` step answers the caller; without one the caller gets 202 as soon as the run waits, or 204 when it ends, and 500 with the reason when it fails.
