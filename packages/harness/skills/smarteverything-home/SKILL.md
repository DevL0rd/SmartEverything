# Home and devices

## Find the target

`get_home` gives room order, providers, people and the home time zone. Narrow `find_devices` by room, family, capability or words (all words must appear in a name, model, kind, family or room; families are exact, so a tower fan is `tower_fan` and a lamp `light`, while kind `fan` covers every fan) and use the returned IDs. When it finds nothing it lists the families in the home: retry with one filter, then ask the person; never guess a device or raw `raw:` ids. Read `get_device` before a new control: capability keys, units, allowed values and danger levels belong to the actual device. If a request fits several devices, resolve that before any physical command. In a text chat the context line `Writing from` names the device the person types on (and a computer's room; a phone has none): use it for "here" and "this room", find that device with `find_devices` words, and ask which room only when it gives none. `get_device` gives each value as `value` and as `shown_as`, the words the app shows (a contact sensor reads Open or Closed).

## Act and report

- `tool_details` gives the live schema; `control_devices` takes one change list across devices, each value in the `value_format` that `get_device` shows for that capability. Colour is a name, hex, RGB 0 to 255 or hue, saturation and value (for example `"#ff0000"` or `{"r":255,"g":0,"b":0}`); brightness, fan speed and a white tone (`color_temperature` in kelvin, `"2700K"` or `"370 mireds"`) are separate capabilities, sent in the same call. Raw `raw:` controls need `deviceIds` and exist only where that device's `get_device` lists them. An action without arguments uses null; one with arguments uses an object matching its schema.
- Check every returned status, error and new value. A pending command is not success. After an unknown outcome do not repeat a physical action automatically.
- `control_device_target` applies one control to a room, named group or explicit selection; use percent for brightness and `scope` `on` to change only members that are on. Groups resolve when the action runs, automations included. See Groups and rooms of lights for reading its result.
- `edit_device` renames, moves, hides or favourites a device, or names a television's inputs with `input_names` (input id to name; the map replaces the old one); `edit_room` and `edit_device_group` organize rooms and groups (removing a room leaves its devices unplaced). To stop one device's or camera's alerts (doorbell, call, alarm, leak, smoke) reaching the person's phones, change their `device_alerts` preference with `update_settings` action preference (to silence only some kinds of one camera's notices, such as motion or person, use the `camera_notifications` preference instead); `edit_device` only changes how the device is shown. `sync_provider` refreshes a provider's device list; it does not repair account linking. A scent diffuser's strength is its `spray_interval` and `spray_duration` set together (Light 20 min and 2 s, Medium 15 min and 4 s, Strong 10 min and 6 s); set the interval first. `spray` sprays once; Spray when someone moves (`auto_fragrance`) and Light when someone moves (`auto_light`) are the diffuser's own motion triggers, separate from SmartEverything automations, and what it sensed shows in `list_events` as `motion` and `no_motion`, its own sprays and lights as `activity`. When the person says they refilled one, call the `recordDeviceRefill` operation (`list_operations`, `call_operation`) for that device: the date is kept for the whole household and shows in `get_settings` under `home.device_refills`.
- IR buttons: names come from `list_remotes`, then `press_remote_button`; an IR press does not prove the appliance changed.
- Phones and watches: `read_phone` or `read_watch` first, then `run_phone_action` or `run_watch_action`. `read_phone` with view context says what a person is doing; with view detail and the phone's deviceId (from `find_devices`) it gives battery and charging, sound and now playing, screen, signal and data use, storage, apps and notifications, next alarm; name the sections wanted to keep it short. What belongs to the phone's own person (apps, notification titles, Wi-Fi name, what is playing) is left out when asking about someone else's phone, so say that instead of guessing. The same cut applies to `get_device`, `get_history`, `list_events` and `find_devices` for someone else's phone: place names, what is playing, screen time and what the phone is doing come back empty or missing for other people. Controlling a phone that is not the person's own is refused for members, and opening an app on a phone works only for its own person, even for the owner; report the refusal as given. Brightness, ringer, volumes, Do Not Disturb and the flashlight are also readable and settable as the phone's capabilities, and so are the screen timeout (`screen_timeout`, seconds as text from 15 to 1800), `auto_rotate`, `haptic_feedback` (touch vibration) and `touch_sounds`; these need the phone's Modify system settings permission, and a refusal naming it is reported as given. `wifi_on`, `bluetooth_on`, `location_on`, `airplane_mode` and `power_save` are read-only: no app can switch Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, mobile data, location, airplane mode, NFC, battery saver or dark mode on Android, so read the state and, only on the person's own phone, send `open_settings` (a capability for `control_devices`, or the `run_phone_action` action of that name) with the page `wifi`, `bluetooth`, `internet`, `location`, `airplane`, `nfc`, `battery_saver`, `display`, `sound`, `dnd_access`, `write_settings` or `app_details` to bring up the right Settings screen, then say plainly that the person must tap the switch themselves. The phone may show a notification to tap instead of opening it at once. Never promise to switch those, and never pass a raw setting name or intent; the pages are a closed list. Finding a phone rings at full volume even on silent; stop it with the same action and on false. `run_phone_action` with receive_file and a chat attachment's id saves that file on the phone (Downloads/SmartEverything); computers take files through `edit_computer_files` upload. Watch actions run through its phone and use the sports it reports. A watch's workout is also a device value: `watch_workout` reads none, running, paused or finished, `watch_workout_start` starts one of the sports it reports and `watch_workout_control` takes pause, resume or finish, so automations can drive the watch with a device step.
- Machine actions are capabilities, not extra tools: ring, stop ringing, open an app, open a link or file, lock, and show the unlock prompt on a phone or computer. `get_device` lists only those the machine reports as usable and says which permission a missing one needs; send them with `control_devices` and report a refusal as given. Unlock on a computer waits for the person's approval on their phone and is refused if nobody approves; a phone cannot be unlocked, only woken to its PIN prompt. Screenshots of a phone or computer use `see`. A refusal names the permission to grant; tell the person where.
- Who or what changed a device, and what a wireless button press ran: the `readDeviceLog` operation (`list_operations`, `call_operation`) lists changes with their cause (button, app and person, automation, AI, schedule, the device itself), presses with the automations they ran, and events, newest first.
- Wireless buttons (Xiaomi) are triggers that send nothing to the appliance: the `pressDeviceButton` operation with `single_press`, `double_press` or `long_press` runs the automations that react to that press and names them. Find a watch with `run_watch_action` and its find action (on true to ring it, false to stop). A scale weigh-in is assigned with `assign_body_reading` (load `smarteverything-health`).
- Home server devices (AdGuard, Syncthing, router, Shelly presence) act through their plugin tools (`read_plugins`, `search_tools`): pause or resume ad blocking with `pause_protection` and `resume_protection`, syncing with its pause tool, and Shelly live tracking with its live view tool (give a holder name, and turn it off again when done).
- Who is home comes from the router plugin (`search_tools`): `who_is_home` lists every known person with home or away, since when, who arrived last and who left last. A person is home when any linked device is on the network (after the router's own grace period against Wi-Fi sleep) or their paired phone reports the place Home; it is for display and automations only, never for choosing the server route. The owner names a network device as a person without an account with `link_device` (`newPerson`, or `person` from `who_is_home`; the MAC comes from the router's device list), links several devices to one person, and uses `manage_person` to add, rename, remove or switch off tracking for one person. A member can read presence but not change it.
- PC RGB lighting (the OpenRGB plugin): each motherboard, RAM, fan, strip or keyboard controller is a `light` device named by OpenRGB, and a controller with several zones also has one light per zone (its `parentExternalId` is the controller). Use `control_devices` with `power`, `brightness`, `color` and, on the controller, `light_effect` (its options are the controller's OpenRGB modes); power off writes black and power on restores the last colour, and colouring a light running an effect switches it to a colour mode first. OpenRGB gives no change notifications for single colours, so a colour set in the OpenRGB window shows after the next device-list update. The lights are offline while the OpenRGB SDK server is not running or the plugin is off (`read_plugins`). Saved profiles belong to the plugin's tools (`read_plugins`, `search_tools`): `status` lists them with the lights, `load_profile` applies one by name, `save_profile` stores the current colours (owner only) and replaces a profile of the same name.
- IR appliance state is assumed, never read back: after every successful press, whoever sent it, the hub remembers for the whole household what the remote last sent (on, temperature, mode, speed, mute, last key and time). Read it in `list_remotes` as `assumed`, in `get_device` under `infrared`, or in `get_settings` under `home.ir_remotes`; a temperature or speed that is null was never sent. When the person says the state is wrong, or names a plug whose power draw shows whether the appliance is really on, call the `updateIrRemote` operation (`list_operations`, `call_operation`) with the remote id (an IR remote device's id or a learned remote's id): it changes only what is remembered and sends nothing to the appliance.
- Explain a measurement with `get_history` for the exact capability and a bounded time range. `get_weather` reads home weather; `search_places` resolves place names without changing settings.

## Groups and rooms of lights

`get_home` lists `device_groups`: every named group and every room with two or more visible lights, each with a `key`, a `target` to pass to `control_device_target`, its `member_ids`, `lost` (members that no longer exist) and live `state` (power all/mixed/off/none, how many are on and online, brightness and white range, colour count, and how many members support each control). Read it before acting so "dim the downstairs lights" hits the right set and a mixed group is described as mixed. To change only what is lit, use `scope` `on`.

`control_device_target` lists `changed`, `failed` and `skipped` members (skipped says offline, not supported or no longer exists) and adds a `headline` such as "2 lights didn't change" when something did not. Report the headline and name each light that did not change with its reason. A member skipped as not supported lacks that control and is not a failure. A command still running has not answered yet, so check `get_device` before saying it worked. Retry only members that failed; an offline light changes when it is back, so say it was offline instead of retrying it.

## Washer-dryer

Read `get_device` first. The machine's remote control must be on (`remote_control` true) or Start fails; report that reason. Send power (if off), the program and the options it uses in one `control_devices` call and check every result succeeded; only then send `cycle_start` in a second call, because commands in one call run in parallel. Changing the program resets its options, so send every option you want. For finish-by set `delay_mode` Finish Wash and `delay_minutes`. Options are locked during a cycle. Use `door_release` only when paused with the door line saying it is safe. Never start Drum Clean with laundry inside. `cycle_stop` needs your confirmation unless the person said stop. Report `remaining_minutes` and the finish time from it.

## Microwave

Read `get_device` first. Every cooking write needs Remote control on, the door closed and the child lock off; a refusal names which, so report it. Set the Cooking mode in its own `control_devices` call, then in the next send the Set cooking duration (an object with `operationTime` as hours:minutes:seconds), Microwave power and Cooking temperature that mode accepts (the capability lists the allowed values; an option the mode lacks is refused with the reason), and only then Start cooking. Some modes can be set but only started on the appliance: say Press Start on the microwave. Pause cooking and Stop cooking need only remote control. Read the Operating state, progress and finish time from `get_device`, and past cooking cycles from the `readDeviceLog` operation.

## Treadmill

A Kingsmith WalkingPad is a device of family `treadmill` (`find_devices` with that family). Read `get_device` first: it gives the belt state (`belt_state`: idle, countdown, running, paused, stopped, standby, safety_stop, fault), the Bluetooth link (`belt_link`: not_linked, searching, connecting, linked, busy, lost), the holder phone, whom it is counting for, the speed limits and the settings the belt has. The belt talks to one phone at a time; `busy` means the vendor app (KS Fit) is holding it and `lost` that the holder phone is out of range, so say that instead of retrying. A command only works while a phone holds the link.

- Control with `control_devices`: `belt_command` start, stop, pause or resume; `belt_speed` in km/h (within the limits `get_device` lists, steps of 0.1; the Kingsmith G15 runs 1 to 15); `belt_mode` (manual, auto, standby); `child_lock`; and, where `get_device` lists them, `belt_max_speed`, `belt_start_speed`, `belt_auto_start`, `belt_sensitivity`, `belt_goal` with `belt_goal_value` (metres, kilocalories or seconds) and `buzzer`.
- A moving belt can hurt someone, so stop and pause are always allowed, but start, resume and any speed-up from you, an automation or another phone are refused unless the owner turned on remote start in the treadmill settings and the caller is the owner (or the walker, if the owner allowed that). Remote speed-ups are capped (6 km/h by default), and a remote start stops itself when no steps arrive within 30 seconds (only on belts that report a step count). Never start the belt, or raise its speed, unless the person asked for exactly that and said they are ready; report a refusal as given and never retry it. Starting from the app needs the person's own one second hold on the phone that holds the link.
- Kingsmith G15 (family `ftms_supplement`, with an adjustable incline): `get_device` and the `readTreadmill` state view show the incline (`belt_incline`, the belt's own number, with its range and step in `limits.incline`), the target incline while the deck moves, the grade, the incline scale (`unconfirmed`, `percent` or `levels`, an owner setting), the climb so far this session in metres, the session energy with its source (`belt` or `estimate`) and the heart rate with its source (`grips` when hands are on the belt grips, else `watch`). While the scale is `unconfirmed` the belt's number has no unit, so say "incline 6" and never "6 percent", and treat climb and energy as a lower estimate.
- Incline from you: `control_devices` sets `belt_incline` to a number in the range (the belt's own number, whole steps from 0 to 20 on the G15; there is no check or lock). Each write moves one step and waits for the belt to confirm; a bigger change is several steps, one after the other. Remote incline (you, an automation or another phone) is off unless the owner turned it on, and the owner's remote incline cap (5 by default) limits only raises; lowering still needs remote incline on and someone walking. It works only for the owner or the person on the belt, never while nobody is walking (no steps seen in the last 30 seconds), never during a fault, and the incline is never moved by a stop, pause or safety stop. Report a refusal as given and never retry it. If another app (KS Fit) holds the belt, say so.
- Power: the G15 cannot be switched off or put to standby over Bluetooth, so never offer a power off. `belt_command` stop halts the belt (the same as Stop on the page, safe at any time); the belt goes to standby by itself after a few idle minutes, and `belt_state` shows `standby` only when the belt itself says so (a belt that dropped the link while stopped may be in standby or switched off, so say that). Switching it off is the power switch on the treadmill.
- Sessions, totals and settings are read and changed with the `readTreadmill` and `actTreadmill` operations (`list_operations`, `call_operation`): `readTreadmill` takes a `view` (state, sessions, session, stats with a `range` of day, week, month or year, records, settings, diagnostics for the owner) and `actTreadmill` an `action` from its schema (who walked a session, whose goals to set, settings including the incline scale and the remote incline policy, marking the belt oiled, which phone holds the link).
- A member sees the live belt (speed, time, distance, steps) but sessions, totals, weight, heart rate and energy only for their own walks; the owner sees everyone's. Energy is an estimate from speed, grade and the walker's weight, and belt steps and distance are counted by the belt, so they can differ from the phone or watch step total.
- The belt reports a panel button press as a `button` event, a fault or the safety key as a `fault` event, and a start, pause, resume, goal and finished walk as `activity` events; `get_history` reads `belt_speed` and the session figures for the last 24 hours by the minute and 30 days by the hour.

## Toothbrush

A Philips Sonicare or an Oral-B iO is a device of family `toothbrush` (`find_devices` with that family). Read `get_device` first: it gives what the handle is doing (`brush_activity`: asleep, standby, brushing, paused, charging, off), the Bluetooth link (`brush_link`: asleep, searching, connecting, linked, busy, needs_pairing, bluetooth_off), the mode, the battery and the brush head. A Sonicare also gives intensity and head wear (`brush_head_wear`); an Oral-B gives the head days left (`brush_head_days_left`), the SmartRing colour (`brush_ring_colour`) and the pacer (`brush_pacer`). Goal progress, streaks, live pressure and force, the quarters reached and the result face are personal and are read with `readToothbrush` (state, stats), never from `get_device`. The brush sleeps most of the time and talks to one phone at a time, so a value can be old: say when it was last seen instead of retrying. `asleep` just means it has not been lifted lately, `busy` means the Philips Sonicare app, the Oral-B app or a charger is holding the brush, and `needs_pairing` that the pairing was lost. Nothing here can start a brush from afar: never claim to, the person lifts it and presses its button.

- A Sonicare handle that writes (the bonded Prestige class, and only after the owner turned on "Let SmartEverything change brush settings") accepts `brush_mode`, `brush_intensity` and, where `get_device` lists them, `brush_feedback_pressure`, `brush_feedback_scrubbing` and `brush_adaptive_intensity` through `control_devices`. A write is refused while the brush is brushing or asleep: tell the person to lift the brush and try again. Every other model is read only; the mode is changed on the handle itself.
- Oral-B brush settings (mode order, pacer, ring colour, head reminder) stay off until a handle test passes on that brush and the owner allowed changes; `get_device` and `control_devices` say which are on. A handle test is a probe (`actToothbrush` with `action` probe, owner only): it talks to the real brush, so ask the person before running one, name what it does, and run it only on a linked, idle brush with the Oral-B app closed.
- Sessions, statistics and settings are read and changed with the `readToothbrush` and `actToothbrush` operations (`list_operations`, `call_operation`): `readToothbrush` takes a `view` (state, sessions, session, stats with a `range` of day, week, month or year, head, battery, handle, settings, diagnostics for the owner) and `actToothbrush` an `action` from its schema (assign a session or the brush to a person, a person's goal, settings, taking over the Bluetooth link, forgetting a session; Oral-B also set_mode_order, reset_head_reminder, snooze_head_reminder and probe). Pairing is a human step in the app.
- Brushings belong to the person the brush is set up for. A member sees that the brush is in use, but sessions, totals, streaks and the score only when they are their own; the owner sees everyone's with `personId`. A Sonicare estimates zones from the timing of the routine; pressure is only "just right" or "too hard".
- On an Oral-B the four quarters are the handle's own 30 second pacer, not where in the mouth the person brushed, and the iO9's 16-zone tracking exists only in the Oral-B app: never describe mouth coverage or say a tooth area was missed. Pressure and force come from the handle while the phone is near; a session brushed away from the phone can be listed without details.
- The brush sends an `activity` event when a brushing is saved, a `maintenance` event when the head is worn or due and a `fault` event for handle errors; `get_history` reads the battery and head wear or days left over time.

## Printers

A network printer (IPP, AirPrint-compatible) is a device of family `printer` (`find_devices` with that family). `get_device` gives its state (`printer_state`: idle, printing, stopped), the queue (`queue`: running or paused), the number of waiting jobs, the problem keyword if it has one, the pages printed today, the lowest supply and the days left, and each supply, tray and output bin as readings. Supplies are cartridges, toner and a waste tank; a level the printer does not report is unknown, never zero. The waste tank is a supply that fills up: it is low when it is nearly full.

- Printing, the queue, history and statistics use the printer plugin's `printer` (read) and `printer_action` tools; find them with `search_tools`. Read views: overview, supplies, trays, queue, jobs, stats, options (what this printer can do and the print settings it accepts), candidates, settings (owner only). Omit `printer` when only one is set up.
- `printer_action` takes an `action`: print (a PDF, JPEG, PNG or WebP from an upload, an attachment or a computer file; ask for copies, sides, colour, quality or paper only when the person said so, and read the options view first so you never send a setting the printer does not list), test_page, cancel, hold and release (a job key), pause and resume the queue, identify (beep or flash so the person can find it), the printer's own web page link, and configure for the caller's own print defaults. Adding a printer, rescanning, forgetting it, cancelling every job, trusting a new certificate, entering its sign-in and clearing its history are owner only; an encrypted printer with a self-signed certificate asks the owner to confirm its fingerprint first. Never ask for or repeat a printer password in chat.
- A member sees and cancels only their own jobs; other people's document names are hidden and the owner sees everyone's. Print only what the person asked for and say what you sent (name, copies, sides). A paused or stopped printer, an empty tray or a jam is reported as given with the plain cause from the problem keyword; do not retry a refused print.
- Ink and toner: `get_history` reads `printer` supply levels over time, the supplies view gives burn rate, days left and a forecast of when it runs out, and a cartridge replacement is a mark on that history. Say when a forecast is rough because there is little history.
- The printer sends plugin events (`job_started`, `job_finished` with the result, `problem_added` and `problem_cleared`, `supply_low`, `supply_empty`, `supply_replaced`, `restarted`, `offline_long`); see `smarteverything-automations`.

## Water bottle

A LARQ PureVis bottle is a device of family `bottle` (`find_devices` with that family). It sleeps and only talks when the cap wakes (a tap, a tilt or a charger), so everything you read is a snapshot with an age: `bottle_link` says asleep, syncing, synced, sync failed or no phone, `last_seen` when a phone last heard it, and `bottle_volume` (ml) and `water_level` (percent) are the last level it measured. Read `get_device` first and say how old the figure is. If `bottle_reliable` is false the level may be off (warm water, tilted, fogged); say so.

- Control with `control_devices`: `hydration_reminder` (off or fixed), `reminder_interval` (15 to 120 minutes, steps of 5), `reminder_from` and `reminder_to` (HH:MM), `reminder_colour`, `quiet_hours` (off, on, auto) with `quiet_from` and `quiet_to`, `uv_clean` (stop only), `bottle_sync` and `measure_level`. Reminder and quiet-hours changes are saved first and applied when the bottle next wakes; say that instead of promising a flash now. `measure_level` and `bottle_sync` need a phone that can reach the bottle and answer "The bottle is asleep. Tap the cap and try again." when it is not awake; relay that and do not retry in a loop. Measuring needs the bottle upright and still.
- You can stop a UV clean but never start one: UV-C needs the person at the bottle, so starting is only the person's own one second hold in the app. Say so when asked to clean the bottle.
- Drinks, fills, sessions, UV cycles, battery, the filter and diagnostics are read and changed with the `readBottle` and `actBottle` operations (`list_operations`, `call_operation`): `readBottle` takes a `view` (status, drinks, readings, sessions, stats with a `range` of day, week, month or year, care, settings, diagnostics) and `actBottle` an `action` from its schema (sync, measure, reminders, quiet hours, bottle size, filter, whose drinks, calibration, taking the link over).
- A bottle belongs to one person: its drinks go into that person's water log and Health Connect. A member sees and changes only their own bottle; the owner sees all. Never bind a bottle to someone else unless the person asked for that exactly. Forgetting a bottle deletes its whole history, so a plugin’s request cannot do it: point the person to the bottle settings in the app.
- The bottle sends `activity` events (drink, refill, uv_clean, goal_reached, drinks_imported), a `fault` event and `maintenance` events (battery_low, filter_due); `get_history` reads `battery`, `bottle_volume` and `water_level`.

## Air, sensors and the desk robot

- Air purifier: Purifier mode (role `purifier_mode`), Favourite level and fan mode are the controls; humidifier: `target_humidity`, `mode` and `mist_level`. Child lock is a setting. Filter life and days left are readings; call `filter_reset` only when the person says the filter was replaced.
- Contact, motion, leak and smoke sensors only report: read them with `get_device`, find when they changed with `get_history` or `list_events` (opened, leak, smoke), and react to them with an automation, never with a command.
- AIBI, the small desk robot, is a device with one control, Move or act: send an object with `action` and `options` through `control_devices`. It moves only while AIBI is in a conversation (the move rides with its next answer) and is refused with that reason otherwise, so `speak` to it or ask the person to talk to it first. Its plugin tools (`read_plugins`, `search_tools`) read its status, what it heard and did, and the moves it offers with their options (read them before choosing a move), and can make it forget its conversations, which only the person's request justifies.

## TVs

Read `get_device` first; its `television` part gives the direct link (`direct_link`: the screen's `address` and the `mac` that wakes it, or null when not linked) and the names given to inputs (`input_names`, input id to name; the TV's own input ids come from the `input_source` options). A linked screen is controlled over the home network first (power, keys including numbers and colours, apps) with SmartThings only as the backup, and it is turned on with Wake-on-LAN to the MAC the link read from the screen itself. A sleeping TV reads off, not broken; `power` on works only when the device has a wake path (the direct link, remote power on while SmartThings sees it, Wake-on-LAN when `wake_address` is set, or an IR remote), otherwise say how to fix it. To link a screen, the person opens that screen's settings in the app while it is on, chooses Link directly, picks the screen and chooses Allow on the screen with its remote; the AI cannot approve that prompt. The `listTelevisionCandidates` operation (`list_operations`, `call_operation`) lists the Samsung screens on the network with their address, MAC and the device each is already linked to. The `findTelevision` operation looks once for a screen that is not linked directly, saves its address when it is certain which screen it is and starts the link so the screen shows its Allow prompt; it does nothing for a linked screen, and the person still chooses Allow on the screen. An offline screen with a `wake_address` and no direct link is woken over the home network and the command succeeds only once it comes online; when it does not, tell the person to turn on Power On with Mobile on the screen (`last_wake_worked_at` says when a wake last worked). Without a direct link or a `wake_address`, suggest linking it directly; only if the person prefers, set the screen's network MAC address (on the TV: Settings, General, Network, Network Status, IP Settings, or About) with `edit_device` (`wake_address`, null clears it); never guess it from another screen. Open an app with one `app_launch`, not key sequences. Navigate with `remote_key` names such as `up`, `ok`, `back`, `home`, `vol_up`, `mute`, `n0` to `n9`, `red`, `green`, `yellow`, `blue`; a key the TV's paths cannot send fails with a reason, so report it. Picture and sound modes are the options `get_device` lists. IR presses are unconfirmed.

Use `show_widgets` for a live device, remote or weather card. A widget a plugin adds is a board type like any other (`pluginId.widgetId`, listed by `read_plugins`): `show_widgets` kind `board` shows it and the board layout operations place it on the Home board.
