# Treadmill, toothbrush and water bottle

These devices sleep or talk to one phone at a time, so a value can be old: say how old it is instead of retrying. Anything that moves or cleans with UV needs the person present: starting the belt, raising its speed or incline and starting a UV clean are refused unless the person asked and the owner allowed it, and a refusal is reported as given, never retried.

## Treadmill

A Kingsmith WalkingPad is a device of family `treadmill` (`device_find` with that family). Read `device_read` first: it gives the belt state (`belt_state`: idle, countdown, running, paused, stopped, standby, safety_stop, fault), the Bluetooth link (`belt_link`: not_linked, searching, connecting, linked, busy, lost), the holder phone, whom it is counting for, the speed limits and the settings the belt has. The belt talks to one phone at a time; `busy` means the vendor app (KS Fit) is holding it and `lost` that the holder phone is out of range, so say that instead of retrying. A command only works while a phone holds the link.

- Control with `device_control`: `belt_command` start, stop, pause or resume; `belt_speed` in km/h (within the limits `device_read` lists, steps of 0.1; the Kingsmith G15 runs 1 to 15); `belt_mode` (manual, auto, standby); `child_lock`; and, where `device_read` lists them, `belt_max_speed`, `belt_start_speed`, `belt_auto_start`, `belt_sensitivity`, `belt_goal` with `belt_goal_value` (metres, kilocalories or seconds) and `buzzer`.
- A moving belt can hurt someone, so stop and pause are always allowed, but start, resume and any speed-up from you, an automation or another phone are refused unless the owner turned on remote start in the treadmill settings and the caller is the owner (or the walker, if the owner allowed that). Remote speed-ups are capped (6 km/h by default), and a remote start stops itself when no steps arrive within 30 seconds (only on belts that report a step count). Never start the belt, or raise its speed, unless the person asked for exactly that and said they are ready; report a refusal as given and never retry it. Starting from the app needs the person's own one second hold on the phone that holds the link.
- Kingsmith G15 (family `ftms_supplement`, with an adjustable incline): `device_read` and the `readTreadmill` state view show the incline (`belt_incline`, the belt's own number, with its range and step in `limits.incline`), the target incline while the deck moves, the grade, the incline scale (`unconfirmed`, `percent` or `levels`, an owner setting), the climb so far this session in metres, the session energy with its source (`belt` or `estimate`) and the heart rate with its source (`grips` when hands are on the belt grips, else `watch`). While the scale is `unconfirmed` the belt's number has no unit, so say "incline 6" and never "6 percent", and treat climb and energy as a lower estimate.
- Incline from you: `device_control` sets `belt_incline` to a number in the range (the belt's own number, whole steps from 0 to 20 on the G15; there is no check or lock). Each write moves one step and waits for the belt to confirm; a bigger change is several steps, one after the other. Remote incline (you, an automation or another phone) is off unless the owner turned it on, and the owner's remote incline cap (5 by default) limits only raises; lowering still needs remote incline on and someone walking. It works only for the owner or the person on the belt, never while nobody is walking (no steps seen in the last 30 seconds), never during a fault, and the incline is never moved by a stop, pause or safety stop. Report a refusal as given and never retry it. If another app (KS Fit) holds the belt, say so.
- Power: the G15 cannot be switched off or put to standby over Bluetooth, so never offer a power off. `belt_command` stop halts the belt (the same as Stop on the page, safe at any time); the belt goes to standby by itself after a few idle minutes, and `belt_state` shows `standby` only when the belt itself says so (a belt that dropped the link while stopped may be in standby or switched off, so say that). Switching it off is the power switch on the treadmill.
- Sessions, totals and settings are read and changed with the `readTreadmill` and `actTreadmill` operations (`operation_call`; `tool_read` gives its input): `readTreadmill` takes a `view` (state, sessions, session, stats with a `range` of day, week, month or year, records, settings, diagnostics for the owner) and `actTreadmill` an `action` from its schema (who walked a session, whose goals to set, settings including the incline scale and the remote incline policy, marking the belt oiled, which phone holds the link).
- A member sees the live belt (speed, time, distance, steps) but sessions, totals, weight, heart rate and energy only for their own walks; the owner sees everyone's. Energy is an estimate from speed, grade and the walker's weight, and belt steps and distance are counted by the belt, so they can differ from the phone or watch step total.
- The belt reports a panel button press as a `button` event, a fault or the safety key as a `fault` event, and a start, pause, resume, goal and finished walk as `activity` events; `history_read` reads `belt_speed` and the session figures for the last 24 hours by the minute and 30 days by the hour.

## Toothbrush

A Philips Sonicare or an Oral-B iO is a device of family `toothbrush` (`device_find` with that family). Read `device_read` first: it gives what the handle is doing (`brush_activity`: asleep, standby, brushing, paused, charging, off), the Bluetooth link (`brush_link`: asleep, searching, connecting, linked, busy, needs_pairing, bluetooth_off), the mode, the battery and the brush head. A Sonicare also gives intensity and head wear (`brush_head_wear`); an Oral-B gives the head days left (`brush_head_days_left`), the SmartRing colour (`brush_ring_colour`) and the pacer (`brush_pacer`). Goal progress, streaks, live pressure and force, the quarters reached and the result face are personal and are read with `readToothbrush` (state, stats), never from `device_read`. The brush sleeps most of the time and talks to one phone at a time, so a value can be old: say when it was last seen instead of retrying. `asleep` just means it has not been lifted lately, `busy` means the Philips Sonicare app, the Oral-B app or a charger is holding the brush, and `needs_pairing` that the pairing was lost. Nothing here can start a brush from afar: never claim to, the person lifts it and presses its button.

- A Sonicare handle that writes (the bonded Prestige class, and only after the owner turned on "Let SmartEverything change brush settings") accepts `brush_mode`, `brush_intensity` and, where `device_read` lists them, `brush_feedback_pressure`, `brush_feedback_scrubbing` and `brush_adaptive_intensity` through `device_control`. A write is refused while the brush is brushing or asleep: tell the person to lift the brush and try again. Every other model is read only; the mode is changed on the handle itself.
- Oral-B brush settings (mode order, pacer, ring colour, head reminder) stay off until a handle test passes on that brush and the owner allowed changes; `device_read` and `device_control` say which are on. A handle test is a probe (`actToothbrush` with `action` probe, owner only): it talks to the real brush, so ask the person before running one, name what it does, and run it only on a linked, idle brush with the Oral-B app closed.
- Sessions, statistics and settings are read and changed with the `readToothbrush` and `actToothbrush` operations (`operation_call`; `tool_read` gives its input): `readToothbrush` takes a `view` (state, sessions, session, stats with a `range` of day, week, month or year, head, battery, handle, settings, diagnostics for the owner) and `actToothbrush` an `action` from its schema (assign a session or the brush to a person, a person's goal, settings, taking over the Bluetooth link, forgetting a session; Oral-B also set_mode_order, reset_head_reminder, snooze_head_reminder and probe). Pairing is a human step in the app.
- Brushings belong to the person the brush is set up for. A member sees that the brush is in use, but sessions, totals, streaks and the score only when they are their own; the owner sees everyone's with `personId`. A Sonicare estimates zones from the timing of the routine; pressure is only "just right" or "too hard".
- On an Oral-B the four quarters are the handle's own 30 second pacer, not where in the mouth the person brushed, and the iO9's 16-zone tracking exists only in the Oral-B app: never describe mouth coverage or say a tooth area was missed. Pressure and force come from the handle while the phone is near; a session brushed away from the phone can be listed without details.
- The brush sends an `activity` event when a brushing is saved, a `maintenance` event when the head is worn or due and a `fault` event for handle errors; `history_read` reads the battery and head wear or days left over time.

## Water bottle

A LARQ PureVis bottle is a device of family `bottle` (`device_find` with that family). It sleeps and only talks when the cap wakes (a tap, a tilt or a charger), so everything you read is a snapshot with an age: `bottle_link` says asleep, syncing, synced, sync failed or no phone, `last_seen` when a phone last heard it, and `bottle_volume` (ml) and `water_level` (percent) are the last level it measured. Read `device_read` first and say how old the figure is. If `bottle_reliable` is false the level may be off (warm water, tilted, fogged); say so.

- Control with `device_control`: `hydration_reminder` (off or fixed), `reminder_interval` (15 to 120 minutes, steps of 5), `reminder_from` and `reminder_to` (HH:MM), `reminder_colour`, `quiet_hours` (off, on, auto) with `quiet_from` and `quiet_to`, `uv_clean` (stop only), `bottle_sync` and `measure_level`. Reminder and quiet-hours changes are saved first and applied when the bottle next wakes; say that instead of promising a flash now. `measure_level` and `bottle_sync` need a phone that can reach the bottle and answer "The bottle is asleep. Tap the cap and try again." when it is not awake; relay that and do not retry in a loop. Measuring needs the bottle upright and still.
- You can stop a UV clean but never start one: UV-C needs the person at the bottle, so starting is only the person's own one second hold in the app. Say so when asked to clean the bottle.
- Drinks, fills, sessions, UV cycles, battery, the filter and diagnostics are read and changed with the `readBottle` and `actBottle` operations (`operation_call`; `tool_read` gives its input): `readBottle` takes a `view` (status, drinks, readings, sessions, stats with a `range` of day, week, month or year, care, settings, diagnostics) and `actBottle` an `action` from its schema (sync, measure, reminders, quiet hours, bottle size, filter, whose drinks, calibration, taking the link over).
- A bottle belongs to one person: its drinks go into that person's water log and Health Connect. A member sees and changes only their own bottle; the owner sees all. Never bind a bottle to someone else unless the person asked for that exactly. Forgetting a bottle deletes its whole history, so a plugin’s request cannot do it: point the person to the bottle settings in the app.
- The bottle sends `activity` events (drink, refill, uv_clean, goal_reached, drinks_imported), a `fault` event and `maintenance` events (battery_low, filter_due); `history_read` reads `battery`, `bottle_volume` and `water_level`.
